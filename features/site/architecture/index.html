How it fits together

Architecture, in six pictures

Each picture has a plain-text twin right under it, so nothing depends on being able to see the drawing. We only draw what the repo's own docs say.

  • 1. Layers and packs
  • 2. Crews and templates
  • 3. Crew Orchestrator flow
  • 4. Eval gate

1. Layers and packs

Three layers, bottom up: a toolbox, a way in, and a one-command setup. If the packs are the cast list, the front door is the call sheet itself.

2. Crews and templates

A crew is a hand-picked cast for a kind of job. Four named ones ship ready; you can also export your own as a file.

3. Crew Orchestrator flow

This is the Crew Orchestrator skill's operating loop, from its own SKILL.md. The part to remember: the gate belongs to someone who did not do the work. A real use case: you ask for a login page, the coordinator confirms which repo you mean, specialists build it on a branch, tests run, and the checker says NEEDS WORK until it sees the evidence itself.

Timeouts and stalls: the skill tells the coordinator to give every external fetch a time box and a fallback route. If the first route stalls past the box, it switches routes and reports what it already has.

4. Eval gate

The gate exists so the library's claims ("this entry is byte-identical to its source", "the board shows what the contract says") are checked by a program rather than by trust. We are honest about its state: the repo's gate record, features/eval/eval/EVAL-GATE.md, records a PASS for the commit it was run on and says it is valid for that commit only. When this docs site was built, a fresh run of run_evals.py on a Windows checkout reported tier 1 and tier 2 FAILs (many of them blob-SHA pin mismatches, possibly caused by line-ending conversion; we did not investigate). So treat the recorded PASS as a statement about that commit, not about your checkout.

5. Live debugger and board

Real scenario: a crew is halfway through a long task and one specialist has gone quiet. Without the board you find out at the end. With it, the quiet agent's card shows silent, and you can nudge it or step in. Everything is asynchronous on purpose: agents just append lines; the board catches up whenever the file changes.

6. Paperclip bridge

If you use Paperclip, you can take a crew you cast here and hand it over as a team folder. It is a small bridge, not a merger.

The Agent Call Sheet

A film crew runs on one sheet of paper that says who is on set, what they do, and what is shooting today. This project does the same for AI agents: a toolbox of skills and personas, a coordinator that runs them like a crew, and a referee who is not allowed to grade their own homework.

What the Agent Call Sheet is, in plain words

It is not one clever chatbot prompt. It is a kit you copy onto your own machine: a library of skills and personas (a code reviewer, a security auditor, a reality checker, SEO jobs, and so on), plus the house rules and the coordinator skill that make a handful of them work as one team. No account, nothing hosted by us runs your crew, and the one optional server it ships runs on your own computer.

Oasis metaphors aside, the idea is simple: the person doing the work should not be the person who declares it finished. Your coordinator splits the job up, specialists do the pieces, and an independent checker starts from "NEEDS WORK" and only moves when there is evidence.

The layers, short version

  1. Library. Skills and personas grouped into six packs, plus a manifest that records, for every entry, where it came from, what license evidence was found, and whether its files are included here or only linked. See the packs.
  2. Front door. A starter prompt with the house rules, the Crew Orchestrator skill that turns your assistant into a crew coordinator, and one install guide per surface. If you install nothing else, install this. See the orchestrator flow.
  3. Bootstrap. One script, features/paperclip-bridge/bootstrap.py, Python standard library only. It copies files into place and nothing more: it fetches nothing and executes nothing. Install steps.

Around those layers sit three helpers. An eval gate checks the library's own claims. A live board lets you watch a crew work in a browser. A Paperclip bridge exports a crew as a team folder. All three are on the architecture page.
